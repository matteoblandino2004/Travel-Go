            How the ranking actually works

            A 5 really outranks a 1

            Your rating becomes a weight of n1.6, normalised across everything you rated. A 5 pulls about 13× as hard as a 1 — on a linear scale, three mid-rated criteria gang up and drown out the one thing you said mattered. Only the ratios count: rating everything 5 ranks the same as rating everything 3.

            N/A means gone, not “low”

            A criterion you mark N/A gets weight zero and is excluded from the score, the explanations, and the breakdown table. It cannot quietly break a tie behind your back — which is what “low priority” does on every other site.

            Scores are relative to what's on screen

            “Cost 5” means cheapest of these options. Each scale is built from the whole result set using Tukey fences, so one £8,000 first-class fare can't squash every real difference between the economy fares. Layover counts and walking minutes use fixed curves instead — a connection costs you the same however good the alternatives are.

            Hotels are scored on where you're going

            Name the sights and restaurants you're actually going for and every hotel is scored on door-to-door travel time to them — 75% weighted average, 25% worst must-see. Without that second term, a hotel on top of four cafés you rated 2 beats one a sensible ride from the museum you rated 5.

            Travel-Go covers every city with an airport. Miles and lounge access are estimated from the fare, cabin and your status — no flight supplier publishes them.
